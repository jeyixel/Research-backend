# Module C model validation and panel defense

**Evidence checked:** existing source and artifacts on 22 October 2026. No models were retrained. The training-table SHA-256 in the evaluation, audit, and extraction artifacts matches the current CSV (`dcbe1bbb358d5f989e4112b6c159c80e02e0378825aa1358f0611ba28918f3e5`). The reported numbers describe this one retrospective public-video corpus and split.

## What was validated

**Target.** For each channel, [the table builder](../../data-collector/build_training_table.py) sorts eligible videos by `published_at`, computes `log_views = log(1 + views)`, and for video *i* takes the median log views of up to 10 preceding and 10 following videos, excluding *i*. At least six neighbors are required. `y_rel_neighbors = log_views_i - neighbor_median_log_views`; [the evaluator](../../creator-performance-backend/app/modules/module_c/evaluation.py) drops missing targets and labels `y = 1` only when that difference is strictly positive. Thus the task is to rank whether a video beat its **local channel baseline**, not to predict CTR, retention, absolute views, or the effect of changing a thumbnail. This target was chosen to reduce the influence of channel size and gradual growth. It is a retrospective label: later videos help set the baseline, and their counts do not establish performance at upload time. The builder defaults also exclude videos under 181 seconds, younger than 30 days at collection, and channels with fewer than 15 eligible videos. These filters do not equalize observation age.

**Separation.** Channel ID is deterministically assigned to folds 0–4 by `MD5(channel_id) % 5`. Fold 4 is the final test; folds 0–3 are development. Within development, each fold is held out in turn for validation while the other three train the model. Candidate logistic-regression `C` values (0.1, 1, 10; constant prior has one setting) are selected by the mean of category AUROCs within each validation fold, averaged across folds. The final model is then fitted on eligible development rows and scored once on fold 4. An image audit removes exact-pixel duplicates, groups near-duplicate thumbnails using a 64-bit difference hash radius of 4, and excludes development rows whose group appears in the test fold. Each development training split also excludes groups in its validation fold; test-related groups are removed from development validation and model selection. The estimator receives only an explicit allowlist of thumbnail measurements, title features/text, category, and/or CLIP image embeddings. Channel ID, fold, subscribers, age, history, and outcomes are excluded from estimator inputs. Preprocessing is inside each fitted pipeline. This is **creator-disjoint evaluation**, but it is not a chronological forecast test: later videos from one creator can have contributed to labels even though that creator stays entirely within one fold.

**Exact sample.** The [audit](../../creator-performance-backend/artifacts/module_c/audit.json) records 31,857 table rows from 313 channels; 1,634 exact image duplicates were excluded, leaving 30,223 usable rows. The [evaluation](../../creator-performance-backend/artifacts/module_c/research/y_rel_neighbors/evaluation.json) reports 24,163 final training rows from 242 channels, 5,989 test rows from **71 distinct channels**, and 71 development rows purged for test-related duplicate groups. The [development predictions](../../creator-performance-backend/artifacts/module_c/research/y_rel_neighbors/development_predictions.csv) contain 24,234 rows, of which 24,163 were eligible for selection. Counts below come directly from the [held-out predictions](../../creator-performance-backend/artifacts/module_c/research/y_rel_neighbors/test_predictions.csv), not from the unfiltered audit distribution.

**Metrics.** [The metric function](../../creator-performance-backend/app/modules/module_c/evaluation.py) uses `predict_proba[:, 1]`. AUROC measures the chance a randomly chosen positive is ranked above a negative (ties share credit). Balanced accuracy is the mean of sensitivity and specificity after thresholding probability at **0.5**. Brier is the mean squared error `(probability - binary_label)^2`, so lower is better; it tests probability quality as well as discrimination. The ordinary test metrics weight videos equally. A second reported set weights each video by the inverse of its channel's test-row count, giving each channel equal total weight. For AUROC uncertainty, the code resamples the 71 test channels with replacement **200 times**, weights each video's predictions by its channel's draw count, drops resamples lacking both classes, and takes the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. Combined-minus-title AUROC uses the **same** resample for both predictions. These are approximate cluster bootstrap intervals for AUROC only; no interval is saved for Brier or balanced accuracy. The interval is conditional on the sampled channels and does not cover label bias, collection bias, or redesign effects.

| Held-out experiment | AUROC | Balanced accuracy | Brier |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Constant-prior baseline | 0.500000 | 0.500000 | **0.250010** |
| Visual measurements | 0.508946 | 0.511161 | 0.250316 |
| Title + category | 0.527420 | **0.525274** | 0.250458 |
| CLIP image embedding | 0.523926 | 0.513264 | 0.256356 |
| Combined | **0.531910** | 0.522669 | **0.275438** |

The combined model's AUROC is 0.031910 above chance and its saved channel-bootstrap 95% interval is **[0.513068, 0.551128]**. Its AUROC exceeds title/category by only **0.004490**, with paired 95% interval **[-0.009148, 0.017295]**: the evidence does **not** establish an incremental benefit from the combined visual inputs over this baseline. Its balanced accuracy is slightly below title/category, and its Brier score is substantially worse than both title/category and the constant-prior baseline. Channel-balanced combined AUROC is 0.527929 and Brier is 0.276500. The development macro AUROC was 0.547661 for combined; the final test AUROC was lower. The artifacts establish weak ranking signal, not a verified reason *why* it is weak. Plausible contributors are noisy view proxies, unequal video ages, unobserved exposure/topic/content, possible title or thumbnail edits, small or inaccurate category/language strata, and a weak relationship between simple packaging features and views. These are hypotheses, not measured cause attribution. The high-dimensional CLIP inputs and selected `C=10` may also contribute to poor probability calibration; that mechanism has not been isolated. Do not claim that 0.532 validates thumbnail design advice or predicted upload uplift.

## Leakage and bias assessment

- The implemented channel split, in-fold preprocessing, explicit input allowlist, and image-group purge address the most direct train/test leakage paths. The audit's near-duplicate groups are **candidates** based on visual hash, not human-confirmed copies; it cannot rule out semantically similar videos or shared creator/network effects across channels.
- The target uses future same-channel videos and collection-time view counts. This is acceptable for a retrospective, channel-disjoint comparison but cannot support a prospective upload-time claim. Videos have different ages despite the 30-day minimum. Current thumbnail/title may differ from what was shown when views accumulated. Median neighbors reduce channel-size effects without removing trends, seasonality, viral events, or exposure differences.
- The [collector](../../data-collector/yt_collector.py) finds candidate channels through chosen search queries, assigns a proposed category from the matched query, and takes at most the latest 200 uploads per selected channel before the table filters. The [collector guide](../../data-collector/README.md) calls for manual category and approval review, but all 356 rows in the current [candidate CSV](../../data-collector/data/candidates.csv) are marked approved; that field alone does not establish individual review. Channel categories and Sri Lankan relevance have not been independently checked in the 90-row [channel review sheet](../../creator-performance-backend/artifacts/module_c/channel_review.csv): its judgment fields are blank. The cohort is therefore a selected sample, not a representative random sample of Sri Lankan creators. A channel-level split does not correct that selection bias.
- The test has 71 channels, but only 13 gaming and 19 cooking channels, and very little Tamil-script coverage. Video-weighted results give larger channels more influence. The channel-balanced results partly address that imbalance, not the domain or label validity.

## Recommendation quality: completed versus pending

**Completed:** the service reports measured image properties and category-relative percentiles; its rules propose checks such as comparing mobile-size readability. It labels the suggestions as design experiments. The [review generator](../../creator-performance-backend/app/modules/module_c/cli.py) prepared **18 fold-4 examples**, six per category with script variety, and automatically checked that recommendation evidence IDs refer to findings. All 18 rows in the [human review sheet](../../creator-performance-backend/artifacts/module_c/review/human_review.csv) have `evidence_ids_valid=True`.

**Pending:** all 18 human judgment fields (`human_findings_supported`, `human_advice_specific`, `human_no_unsupported_claims`, `human_neighbors_relevant`) and notes are blank. The [audit](../../creator-performance-backend/artifacts/module_c/audit.json) also marks human review pending. There is no recorded human accuracy or usefulness rate, local-script readability check, inter-rater agreement, A/B redesign study, CTR validation, or observed recommendation uplift. Evidence-ID validity is a structural check, not proof that a finding is perceptually true or useful. The 18 examples were selected for category/script coverage and are not a representative random estimate of product accuracy.

## Concise answer to the panel

> We evaluated a research-only Module C classifier on 5,989 public videos from 71 entirely held-out creator channels. The label was whether a video's log views exceeded the median of nearby videos from its own channel; it is a relative-view proxy, not CTR or the effect of a thumbnail change. We chose model settings on four separate development folds, purged duplicate-thumbnail groups across splits, and compared against constant-prior, title/category, visual-only, and CLIP baselines. The combined model reached 0.532 AUROC, but its gain over title/category was only 0.0045 with a channel-bootstrap interval crossing zero, and its Brier score was worse than the constant baseline. This is weak retrospective signal, not evidence of accurate outcome forecasts. Our feedback rules are tied to measured features and have passed evidence-link checks, but human judgments of correctness and usefulness are still pending. Before the presentation we can rate the 18 prepared examples with two reviewers and report the pass counts and failures. We cannot yet claim that the recommendations improve clicks or views.
